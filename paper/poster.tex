%% ---------------------------------------------------------------- row 1
\begin{columns}
\column{0.34}
\block{The question}{
  \large
  Much of bioinformatics is not discovery. It is converting a GTF to a BED,
  checking a BAM is coordinate-sorted, normalising counts to TPM --- precisely
  specified, tedious, easy to get subtly wrong.
  \vspace{0.6em}

  So: \textbf{which parts can be delegated to an agent, and how often is the
  result right?}
  \vspace{0.6em}

  Answering that needs a benchmark that (i)~runs the tools rather than
  describing them, (ii)~grades deterministically --- no LLM judge --- and
  (iii)~runs on a laptop or an institutional node, because the data that most
  needs help often cannot leave the building.
}

\column{0.32}
\block{What the benchmark is}{
  \large
  \textbf{32 tasks}: a 25-task core plus a 7-task extension of multi-stage
  workflows. Each gives the agent real files, a container with the usual stack,
  and an exact output contract. Grading compares the produced artifact against
  a stored correct answer.
  \vspace{0.5em}

  \centering
  \small
  \begin{tabular}{lr@{\hspace{2em}}lr}
    \toprule
    alignment & 7 & search & 5 \\
    variant & 7 & RNA-seq & 4 \\
    metadata & 6 & \multicolumn{2}{l}{} \\
    quality control & 6 & \multicolumn{2}{l}{} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{0.4em}

  \raggedright
  \large Fixed agent (Terminus-2) and harness (Harbor) across all models, so
  score differences reflect the model, not the scaffolding.
}

\column{0.34}
\block{Three headline results}{
  \vspace{0.2em}
  \centering
  \headline{btblue}{0.969}{best mean reward on the core --- \textbf{the original suite is saturated}}
  \headline{btorange}{44\%}{of failures are well-formed files with \textbf{wrong numbers}}
  \headline{btviolet}{9/32}{tasks solved on \textbf{every} attempt by \textbf{every} model}
  \vspace{0.6em}
}
\end{columns}

\begin{columns}
\column{0.5}
\block{Difficulty lives in reliability, not in what a model can eventually do}{
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.70\linewidth]{figures/fig_paired_scopes.pdf}
  \vspace{0.4em}

  \raggedright
  \large Each row is one model: blue = core, orange = extension.
  \vspace{0.4em}

  \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=btblue!8,colframe=btblue,boxrule=1.2pt,arc=3mm]
  \large\textbf{Best-of-four hides the gap.} All five models of the OpenRouter
  cohort solve \emph{every} extension task within four attempts and are
  therefore indistinguishable. Under the \textbf{all-four} criterion --- solved
  on \emph{all} attempts, the one that matters for unattended analysis --- they
  span \textbf{2/7 to 7/7}.
  \end{tcolorbox}
}

\column{0.5}
\block{How agents fail --- and why it matters}{
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{figures/fig_failure_modes.pdf}
  \vspace{0.4em}

  \raggedright
  \large All 319 scored failures, classified from the verifiers' own reasons.
  \vspace{0.4em}

  \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=btorange!8,colframe=btorange,boxrule=1.2pt,arc=3mm]
  \large\textbf{The largest category is the quiet one.} A BED with correct
  syntax and wrong coordinates, or a flagstat summary with plausible but
  incorrect counts, propagates silently into whatever consumes it. Spread
  across ordinary tasks --- \texttt{blast-best-hit}, \texttt{gtf-to-bed},
  \texttt{flagstat-report} --- not exotic ones.
  \end{tcolorbox}
}
\end{columns}

\block{What hardware do you actually need?}{
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.78\linewidth]{figures/fig_hardware_vs_score.pdf}
  \vspace{0.5em}

  \small
  \begin{tabular}{llr}
    \toprule
    Tier & Hardware & Example \\
    \midrule
    A & laptop / workstation (4-bit) & Gemma 4, 62\,GB \\
    B & one datacentre GPU & Qwen3.5 122B, 122\,GB \\
    C & 2--4 GPU server & DeepSeek V4 Flash, 304\,GB \\
    D & full 8-GPU node & GLM 5.2, 756\,GB \\
    E & multi-node cluster & Kimi K3, 2.8\,TB \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \vspace{0.5em}

  \raggedright
  \large
  Bigger is better on average ($\rho=0.77$) but \textbf{saturates above
  $\sim$130\,GB}: a 62\,GB workstation model outscores a 122\,GB one needing a
  datacentre GPU.
}

\column{0.5}
\block{Cost, openness, and what to delegate}{
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.76\linewidth]{figures/fig_cost_capability.pdf}
  \vspace{0.4em}

  \raggedright
  \large \textbf{Open weights and low price are independent.} The cheapest run
  is also tied for the best score; the dearest costs $\sim$13$\times$ more for
  the same result.
  \vspace{0.5em}

  \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=btviolet!8,colframe=btviolet,boxrule=1.2pt,arc=3mm]
  \large\textbf{Delegate today:} format conversion and summarisation ---
  GTF$\rightarrow$BED, VCF$\rightarrow$TSV, FASTA statistics, flagstat counting
  --- with spot-checking.\\[0.3em]
  \textbf{Do not yet:} multi-stage reasoning over biological structure ---
  splice-junction support, trio-aware and diploid-consensus variant logic.\\[0.3em]
  \textbf{Rule of thumb:} delegation is safe in proportion to how mechanically
  checkable the output is.
  \end{tcolorbox}
}
\end{columns}

%% ---------------------------------------------------------------- row 4
\begin{columns}
\column{1}
\block{Take home}{
  \large
  \begin{minipage}[t]{0.30\linewidth}
    \textbf{Routine terminal bioinformatics is largely automatable today}, by
    models a laboratory can host itself --- a workstation-class 31B model
    already solves most of the core suite.
  \end{minipage}\hfill
  \begin{minipage}[t]{0.30\linewidth}
    \textbf{Report mean reward and all-attempts, not best-of-$n$.} Best-of-four
    describes a workflow with an oracle telling you which run was right. You do
    not have one --- that is why you wanted the agent.
  \end{minipage}\hfill
  \begin{minipage}[t]{0.34\linewidth}
    \textbf{Check the output, not the score.} When these agents fail, the most
    common single outcome is a plausible, well-formed file containing wrong
    numbers --- the failure mode least likely to be caught downstream.
  \end{minipage}
